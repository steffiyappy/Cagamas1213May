Treasury & Capital Markets Build Pack

🤖 Agent: "HHFC Market Desk"

🏢 About Your Division

HHFC's Treasury & Capital Markets division raises capital through bond and sukuk issuances and manages investor relations. The team spends 4 days per issuance rebuilding investor documents from scratch, leaving no bandwidth for market monitoring. An investor-facing agent could handle routine queries, freeing the team for higher-value work.

🤖 Agent Brief: HHFC Market Desk

Purpose: Support HHFC's Treasury team and external investors by providing quick answers on HHFC's outstanding securities, upcoming issuances, market context, and investor FAQ — reducing the documentation burden on the team during live issuance windows.

Primary Users: Institutional investors, fund managers, and internal Treasury team members seeking quick reference information.

Core Problem it Solves: During each issuance window, the team receives 11–31 investor queries and can answer fewer than 50% on the same day. An agent handling standard queries would dramatically improve investor response times and free the team to monitor markets.

💬 Sample Conversations

Scenario 1 — Outstanding Securities
What bonds does HHFC currently have outstanding?
🤖 HHFC Market Desk HHFC (Harmoni Housing Finance Corporation) currently has the following categories of outstanding securities: Cagamas Notes (conventional), Cagamas Sukuk (Islamic), Green Bonds, and Sustainability Sukuk. For specific current outstanding amounts, maturities, and coupon rates, please refer to the latest investor fact sheet available on the HHFC investor relations portal, or I can connect you with the Treasury team for a current schedule. Is there a specific instrument type you are evaluating?
Scenario 2 — Credit Rating
What is HHFC's credit rating and what does it mean for investors?
🤖 HHFC Market Desk HHFC carries a AAA local currency credit rating — the highest rating available from Malaysian rating agencies. As Malaysia's national housing finance corporation operating under Bank Negara Malaysia's oversight, HHFC carries an implied sovereign backing that underpins this rating. For investors, this means: (1) lowest credit risk in the Malaysian corporate bond market, (2) eligible for LCR Level 2A/2B treatment at most financial institutions, (3) typically priced at a modest spread above Malaysian Government Securities. The AAA rating reflects HHFC's policy mandate, not simply its financial metrics.
Scenario 3 — Investment Risks
We are considering HHFC's next sukuk. What are the key risks?
🤖 HHFC Market Desk Key risk considerations for HHFC sukuk investors include: (1) Prepayment Risk — underlying mortgages may prepay faster than expected, affecting yield duration; (2) Interest Rate / Profit Rate Risk — value of fixed-rate securities will vary inversely with OPR movements; (3) Liquidity Risk — secondary market for HHFC securities is moderately liquid; (4) Credit Risk — minimal given AAA rating, but note HHFC is not explicitly guaranteed by the Malaysian government. Full risk factors are disclosed in the issuance prospectus. Would you like me to help draft a risk question list for your investment committee?
Scenario 4 — Investor FAQ Draft
Can you draft a brief investor FAQ for our upcoming green bond issuance?
🤖 HHFC Market Desk Here is a draft 5-question investor FAQ for the upcoming HHFC Green Bond issuance:

Q1. What is the use of proceeds? Proceeds will fund the purchase of mortgages secured on green-certified residential properties meeting BCA or GBI standards.
Q2. What is the expected yield range? Indicative pricing will be announced at launch; historically HHFC green bonds have priced at 15–25bps premium to conventional notes.
Q3. What green certification framework applies? HHFC's Green Finance Framework is aligned with ICMA Green Bond Principles and externally reviewed.
Q4. What is the expected tenor? Typically 3–7 years, subject to market conditions.
Q5. What ESG reporting commitments does HHFC make? Annual green bond impact reports detailing use of proceeds and environmental KPIs.
