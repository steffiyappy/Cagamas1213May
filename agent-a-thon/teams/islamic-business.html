🤖 Agent: "HHFC Shariah Guide"

🏢 About Your Division

HHFC's Islamic Finance division manages Islamic mortgage asset purchases, sukuk issuances, and the Islamic Mortgage Guarantee Programme (MGP-i). The division works closely with the Shariah Advisory Committee. The team spends significant time on manual compliance lookups before each issuance — time that a well-built knowledge agent could dramatically reduce.

🤖 Agent Brief: HHFC Shariah Guide

Purpose: Support HHFC's Islamic Finance team's internal workflow by providing quick answers on Shariah compliance requirements, sukuk product structures, and common AAOIFI standards — reducing the manual lookup burden before each issuance.

Primary Users: Islamic Finance team members preparing sukuk issuance documentation, Shariah compliance officers, and internal teams seeking Islamic finance guidance.

Core Problem it Solves: The team spends 12–15 hours per issuance on manual Shariah compliance preparation. An agent that serves as an always-available Shariah reference and checklist generator could reduce preparation time by 40–60%.

💬 Sample Conversations

Scenario 1 — Murabahah Sukuk Requirements
What are the key Shariah requirements for a Murabahah-based sukuk?
🤖 HHFC Shariah Guide For a Murabahah-based sukuk, the key Shariah requirements are: (1) Valid Underlying Asset — a real, tangible asset must be identified and purchased; (2) Valid Aqad — the offer and acceptance (Ijab and Qabul) must be properly documented; (3) No Guaranteed Return — profit must be tied to the sale transaction, not guaranteed independently; (4) Shariah Advisory Committee approval before issuance; (5) Full disclosure in offering documents. Note: I recommend verifying the final checklist with your Shariah Advisor before submission to the SAC.
Scenario 2 — AAOIFI Standards
Is a floating profit rate permissible under AAOIFI standards for our Musharakah sukuk?
🤖 HHFC Shariah Guide Under AAOIFI standards, floating profit rates in Musharakah structures are permissible under certain conditions: the rate must be linked to a permissible benchmark (e.g., KLIBOR or SOFR-equivalent, not a guaranteed fixed return), and the rate mechanism must not create a guaranteed return that converts the Musharakah into a loan arrangement. Key condition: the floating rate must be applied to the profit distribution, not to the capital. I strongly recommend your Shariah Advisor reviews the specific rate mechanism before finalising documentation, as AAOIFI Standard 12 provides the definitive guidance.
Scenario 3 — Compliance Checklist
What items should be in the Shariah compliance checklist for our next issuance?
🤖 HHFC Shariah Guide Here is a standard 10-item Shariah compliance checklist for an HHFC sukuk issuance:
1. Underlying asset Shariah screening completed ✓
2. Aqad (offer & acceptance) language verified ✓
3. Profit rate structure reviewed against relevant fatwas ✓
4. English prospectus vs Arabic fatwa alignment confirmed ✓
5. Wakalah appointment letter language reviewed ✓
6. SAC meeting minutes referenced in documentation ✓
7. Portfolio concentration limits verified ✓
8. Investor disclosure on Islamic risk factors included ✓
9. Dissolution event clauses reviewed ✓
10. Prior SAC resolutions cross-referenced ✓
Would you like me to generate this as a working document template?
Scenario 4 — Dissolution Clause Issue
Our Shariah advisor flagged an issue with the dissolution clause. What should we check?
🤖 HHFC Shariah Guide Dissolution clause issues in sukuk are typically flagged for one of three reasons: (1) the clause creates a guaranteed purchase obligation that converts the structure into a loan; (2) the dissolution trigger events are not aligned with Shariah principles; or (3) the purchase price at dissolution creates a guaranteed return. Could you share the specific clause text? I can help you identify which of these issues applies and suggest standard language that SACs have previously accepted. This review should still be confirmed by your qualified Shariah Advisor before resubmission.
